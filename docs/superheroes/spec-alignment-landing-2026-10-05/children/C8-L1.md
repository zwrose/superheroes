What: The card and its answers, on C7's shell and theme. Every card except a final sheet's last card has the same parts in this order: the kind of call; the question in one line, in the owner's words where possible; the context (what's true now, why it needs the owner, the exact text the call is about); images when the call has relevant ones (their viewing is layer 4's); options when there are real ones, each with its plain consequence; a one-line recommendation with its reason; the owner's answer; a note. Each offers Aligned and Discuss, plus a direct pick when it has options; Aligned agrees with the recommendation (or the statement when there is none), a pick chooses that option, Discuss leaves the call open for chat, and no review adds its own answer buttons. A tap saves at once as a draft the owner can change, the last tap counting, into the sheet's own store (R18); a failed save says so on the card and lets the owner try again. A sheet is private to the owner by default; the owner may share it, and anyone else who opens it sees it but cannot answer. A usage doc, alongside C7's publishing doc, says any session may use the template when it has calls for the owner, and names no expected use beyond discovery's sheets; that the sending session reads every answer together once the owner says the sheet is done and never acts on a single tap; that a note disagreeing with its answer is read as Discuss; and the chat-prose fallback where the host can't show a sheet (R20). **Register entries this layer consumes (quoted verbatim from docs/superheroes/spec-alignment-landing-2026-10-05/register.md):**

> **R17 — The sheet data file.** A sheet is the plugin's review template published together with one data file that holds the sheet's cards; no session edits the template's code to make a sheet, and the data file is also the sheet's saved source. The data file names the sheet's kind (a remainder sheet, a final sheet, or a plain sheet for any other review) and its title; for a remainder sheet, the facts behind "why only these" (rounds run, fixes made, any finding the review didn't settle); for a final sheet, how the spec got there (review rounds, fixes, the vet), the folded declined findings, and the approval card's facts (whether there is an approved board and whether it is saved with the spec). Each card carries a stable id, the kind of call, whether it carries the warning badge, the question, its context (what is true now, why it needs the owner, the exact text), its images, its options each with a plain consequence, and a one-line recommendation with its reason. The data file's field names are decide-by: C7, which lands the schema with the template.

> **R18 — How answers come back.** A sheet is published private to the owner, and its own store holds the answers: each tap writes or replaces one document per card, keyed by the card's id, in an `answers` collection (the answer, which is Aligned, Discuss or an option, and the note), so the last tap counts; a final sheet's verdict (Approve or Not yet, with its note) is one separate document written only by "Send verdict". The session that sent the sheet reads every answer together once the owner says the sheet is done (on a final sheet, "Send verdict" is that word), never acts on a single tap, and treats a card whose note disagrees with its answer as Discuss. Another session can take a sheet over by reading its data file and its store; nothing assumes the owner has a second account.

> **R19 — The theme ships once.** The Comic panel theme ships as one stylesheet, with its fonts declared, at a path C7 names; the review template and every board's frame and labels load it, nothing else restates a theme colour, font or part rule, and a board draws the product itself in the project's design system, never in the theme. The path is decide-by: C7.

> **R20 — When a sheet can't be shown.** Where the host can't show a sheet, the session puts the same cards to the owner as numbered chat prose, each with its context, options and recommendation, built from the same data file; the template's usage doc states this once and discovery points at it.

> **R24 — Glossary terms.** The glossary, `plugins/superheroes/rubric/glossary.md`, gains one entry each, under a stable heading, for Canon, ceded call and standing ruling (C1); the owner-vs-craft line (C2); remainder sheet and ready for vet (C4); and card and sheet (C8). Every other surface links the heading and states no definition of its own.

> **R25 — Shipped text carries no spec provenance.** Plugin text this epic ships states its rules without the specs' source tags, ruling numbers, requirement numbers or discovery-note references, per `plugins/superheroes/rubric/prose-standard.md` § "Shipped surfaces carry no project provenance"; source tags belong in specs, never in shipped doctrine.
