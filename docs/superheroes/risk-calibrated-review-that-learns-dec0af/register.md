# Contract register — risk-calibrated-review-that-learns-dec0af

Cross-child technical decisions for the epic that lands the spec the owner approved on 2026-10-10,
`risk-calibrated-review-that-learns-dec0af` (committed beside this file, `spec.md`). The entries are
numbered, and each is a plain binding sentence with its consuming children named. Every entry is
either decided now or names the child that decides the detail (**decide-by**). The advisor owns
edits; a builder never amends the register it is graded against. Children quote each entry's first
paragraph **verbatim**. The `*Source:*`, `*Status:*` and `*Consumers:*` lines are trailer and are
not quoted. Register entries stay quoted on a stacked child's feature issue; its layer bodies name
the entries that bind them, and a layer's register check runs on the feature issue's body with the
feature's token (`skills/showrunner/reference/register-check.md` § Stack layer inputs).

No entry adds product opinion. Where a decision comes from the spec, the `*Source:*` line names the
spec text, and an owner ruling is quoted as a dated copy that names its Canon entry id
(`docs/superheroes/canon.md`). Where the spec is silent, the entry marks the gap **decide-by**, and
the named child records its choice for the owner's veto. If the choice would change what the owner
sees, is asked or keeps, the child raises it as an owner call instead.

**R1 — The review record.** When a review ends, on every lane (full, light and micro), the plugin writes exactly one review record for the PR, through one record writer in `plugins/superheroes/lib/` that every lane's end-of-review step calls and no other surface duplicates. The record holds: the lane and why the change got it; which reviewers were planned and which ran, each with its model family; every finding the reviewers raised, with its stated consequence, its outcome and the reason for that outcome; the final commit and the CI result on it; what is left for the owner; the number of rounds, and whether the loop stopped at its round cap; any planned review that did not run, with the owner's ruling or word that let the PR go ahead without it, if there is one; and the cost. Facts that code holds are taken from code, never from a session's account: the lane, the final commit, the check results, and, for each reviewer an engine ran, that it ran, read from the engine's own record. Where only the session that ran the review reports a reviewer's run, the record says "reported by the session" beside that reviewer. The record keeps every finding, craft decisions and findings shown wrong included, readable after the PR merges, and the PR names its record so the advisor's vet, the sheet builder and a misses-log entry each reach it in one hop. No record says that a change has no bugs. The record's file shape, its home for each PR, how the PR names it, and the unit the cost is stated in are decide-by: C1.

**R3 — Risk and trust, and how the loop reads it.** Configure holds one item named "Risk and trust": the project's existing threat model (`threatModel` in `plugins/superheroes/lib/project_config.py`), unchanged, plus a list of risk areas, each naming which parts of the project it covers, why it is risky, and how hard a mistake there is to undo. The covered parts are stored in a form code can match against a change, and one reader in `plugins/superheroes/lib/` answers which risk areas a change touches; every consumer (the lane call, the panel, the test role) calls that reader and none re-derives the match, so a change that plainly sits inside an area is matched with no model judgment. When the item is missing, holds no risk areas, or cannot be read, the reader reports the risk areas as not set, and the plugin then gives every change whose risk is unclear the full lane and brings every leftover with a real consequence to the owner. Whether the item keeps the threat-model item's number and slug, the stored shape of a risk area, the matching form and the reader's interface are decide-by: C4; what makes a change's risk unclear when no risk areas are set is decide-by: C5, recorded for the owner's veto.
*Source:* FR-9 and its bullet; FR-10 and its first bullet; FR-78 and its bullet; UFR-1 and its two bullets; Canon 2026-10-09-d6064e1d-2 (ruled 2026-10-09), quoted: "The risk profile is part of one configure item, Risk and trust: today's threat model, extended with structured risk areas (which parts of the project, why, how reversible) that the review loop reads mechanically."
*Status:* decided; **decide-by: C4** for the item's number and slug, the stored shape, the matching form and the reader's interface; **decide-by: C5** for what counts as unclear when no risk areas are set.
*Consumers:* C4, C5, C6, C7.

**R4 — A finding, its consequence and its five outcomes.** Every finding states its consequence if it is left unfixed, and a finding with no stated consequence is not taken into the review. A finding whose only consequence is code quality is craft, wherever it sits, a risk area included; any other consequence is a real consequence (`plugins/superheroes/rubric/glossary.md` § Material consequence). Every finding ends with exactly one of five outcomes, recorded with its reason in the review record: fixed; shown wrong; decided as craft; left for the owner; or decided by a named ruling (a standing ruling in the project's Canon, or a recorded residual under `plugins/superheroes/rubric/review-discipline.md` § Review bars and recorded residuals). Severity only orders the work and never changes an outcome. On a PR sheet, findings decided as craft sit under "Decided as craft, for your veto", findings left for the owner under "What you're accepting", and findings decided by a ruling under "Already decided" with the ruling named; "How the review went" carries the review's history, as the approved board draws it. The findings contract's consequence field and the outcomes' spelling in the record are decide-by: C1, which lands them in the seam; C7 makes a finding without a consequence refused.
*Source:* FR-30 to FR-34; FR-36; FR-69; UFR-2; Glossary (Real consequence, Craft, Leftover); Canon 2026-10-09-d6064e1d-4 (ruled 2026-10-09), quoted: "Every review finding states its consequence if left unfixed. A finding with no consequence beyond code quality is craft: the loop decides it, records it for the owner's veto and keeps it retrievable. A real consequence left unfixed goes to the owner by default, until the project's own standing rulings set a bar that clearly decides the case. Severity only orders the work."; Canon 2026-10-10-d6064e1d-16 (ruled 2026-10-10), quoted in part: "A finding with no consequence beyond code quality is craft wherever it sits, a risk area included".
*Status:* decided; **decide-by: C1** for the consequence field and the outcome spellings.
*Consumers:* C1, C6, C7, C9.

**R5 — Independence: who may review.** Every reviewer of a change comes from a different model family than every maker of that change. A maker is any model that built or fixed the product's code under review, so a fixer's family joins the makers for every later round; a reviewer that writes break-it tests for a change is not a maker of it. When no reviewer from a different family than every maker can run, the review counts as not run, as for a planned reviewer that did not run, and no session picks a same-family reviewer or stands in for the reviewer on its own. Only the owner's word, for that one change, lets a reviewer from a maker's family run, and the review record then names that reviewer and says the review was not independent.
*Source:* FR-19 and its two bullets; UFR-4 and its three bullets; Glossary (Maker); Canon 2026-10-10-d6064e1d-12 (ruled 2026-10-09, recorded 2026-10-10), quoted: "Independence means reviewers from a different model family than every maker. Entry 2026-10-10-d6064e1d-5 says who counts as a maker."; Canon 2026-10-10-d6064e1d-5 (ruled 2026-10-10), quoted: "A reviewer's own break-it tests do not make that reviewer a maker of the change."
*Status:* decided. Cross-epic note (advisor, 2026-10-10): the cloud-builders package (`docs/superheroes/cloud-builders-66a2e8/`) builds on this entry unchanged; see that register's R7. The seam is one-directional: this entry takes no input about where a build ran.
*Consumers:* C1, C5, C6, C7.

**R6 — A single reviewer's reading, from the engine's own record.** On the light and micro lanes the planted-bug probe is gone (C2), and in its place the review record shows, from the engine's own record of the run, that the one reviewer read the change. A record that is missing, or that cannot tell whether the reviewer read the change, counts as not showing it; then the review counts as not run, the plugin runs the reviewer once more, and if the record still does not show a reading the change moves up to the full lane or parks. Which field of an engine's own record shows a reading, and how the choice between moving up and parking is made, are decide-by: C5.
*Source:* FR-18 and its three bullets; FR-5's second bullet; Canon 2026-10-08-d6064e1d-19 (ruled 2026-09-28), quoted: "The planted-bug review control retires everywhere in the plugin; the engine wiring check and the new-model admission check stay, as different jobs."
*Status:* decided; **decide-by: C5** for the record field and the move-up-or-park choice.
*Consumers:* C1, C2, C5.

**R7 — The four answer patterns.** Every question on a PR sheet and on the index uses one of the four accepted answer patterns, and no PR sheet or index offers a button outside them: pick for a batch-1 question and for an item with a real choice; agree, with "Accept" as its yes-word, for an item with no real choice; check for a walk stop; and sign-off for the merge verdict. Discovery's sheets keep the buttons they have. The owner's standing ruling, Canon 2026-10-10-d6064e1d-17 (ruled 2026-10-10), quoted: "Every question on any sheet uses one of four accepted answer patterns, and the sheet's author picks the one that fits the question. Agree: a yes-word plus Discuss, where the yes-word is Aligned on a recommendation and Accept on a leftover the owner is taking on. Pick: one button per option, plus Something else. Check: Works, Problem and Skip for something the owner tries, with Looks right in place of Works when the owner only looks, and Problem then asks whether to fix it before merge or in a follow-up. Sign-off: one action plus Not yet, Approve on a spec and Merge on a pull request, and it may stay locked until the rest of the sheet is answered. Every pattern keeps the optional note box. Adding a pattern takes the owner's ruling. A card keeps the parts entry 2026-10-05-3dbeb858-27 gave it: context, images, options, recommendation and note. This replaces that entry's rule of one card shape with no per-review buttons."
*Source:* FR-55's fifth, sixth and seventh bullets; FR-43; FR-52; FR-53; FR-56; FR-57; FR-59; FR-62 to FR-66; What this spec changes elsewhere (the review surface spec's FR-6).
*Status:* decided.
*Consumers:* C8, C9.

**R8 — The owner's word to merge.** The owner's word to merge a PR is a saved "Merge" verdict on that PR's current sheet together with the owner saying "done" in the chat; neither alone is the word, and a saved "Not yet", or no saved verdict, together with "done" is not the word. When the PR's head commit moves after its sheet was published, the sheet is replaced by an unsigned one for the new commit, which shows "This PR changed", keeps the answers to unchanged items and starts the merge verdict over, so a verdict saved on the old sheet never counts for the new commit. A "Fix in a follow-up" answer is the owner's word to file that follow-up.
*Source:* FR-66 and its two bullets; FR-67; UFR-6 and its bullet; Canon 2026-10-09-d6064e1d-8 (ruled 2026-10-09), quoted in part: "A saved merge verdict plus the owner saying done in the chat is the merge word, and a fix-in-a-follow-up answer is the owner's word to file that follow-up."; Canon 2026-10-09-d6064e1d-7 (ruled 2026-10-09), quoted in part: "The sheet names the PR's head commit and is republished unsigned when the head moves, and the answers and verdict are written back to the PR as its receipt."

One sheet builder makes a PR sheet's data file from two inputs only: the PR's review record (R1), whose facts and finding outcomes it shows without re-deriving or adding to them, and the advisor's inputs for that sheet (the batch-1 questions and their pictures, the merge order, the walk's stops, which stops were walked and how, why a stop was not walked, and the items beyond the leftovers that need the owner, such as a spec's closure receipt). The sheet carries every picture inside its own published files, never a link to another page, and a picture that cannot be copied shows as missing on its stop. The data file's shape, the new sheet kinds in `plugins/superheroes/theme/sheet.schema.json`, and the shape of the advisor's input are decide-by: C9; the advisor's doctrine (C8) points at that shape and restates none of it.
