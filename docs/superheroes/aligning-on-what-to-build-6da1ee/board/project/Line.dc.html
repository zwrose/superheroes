The owner-vs-craft line, in full

One line for the whole plugin and every project that uses it: discovery, the advisor, merges, fixes after approval, deviations and follow-ups. Examples are real calls from your sessions. When in doubt, it's yours.

Yours · the ten owner categories
1 Who the product is for, and what it's for
The people the product serves and the job it does for them, kept once per project. Every other call leans on it, so discovery reads it first.
  • superheroes is for a product-minded owner who isn't expected to know engineering craft.
  • Weekly Eats is for households planning meals and shopping together, and it's headed to a paid product.
Not yours when a spec just restates what's already recorded.
2 Who this piece of work is for, and what they're trying to do
Told as user stories: "As a …, I want …, so I can …", captured in each discovery. They evoke the core need, not every detail; the requirements carry the detail. It's what tells two right-looking designs apart.
  • #1798: As a household shopper, I want to add more of something than my meal plans need, so I can stock up without losing track of what the plans need.
  • #1798: As a shopper mid-aisle, I want one number on each row, so I can put the right amount in the cart without doing the sums.
Not yours when the approved board or an agreed story already shows it. Edge cases are requirements, not stories.
3 What a person sees or can do at a given moment
The behaviour of one screen or step: what's shown, what can be tapped, what happens next, and what happens when it goes wrong.
  • #1798: Remove on a row that plans feed refuses, and points to Plans.
  • #1798: a row showing ??? can't be checked off; tapping it opens its question.
Not yours when it matches an approved board or an existing pattern exactly.
4 Whether a thing should exist at all
Scope, and whether it's worth what it costs to build and keep, including retiring something already built.
  • #1419: no "test version" concept in any environment; document versions are just seed data.
  • #1798: no extra controls for the rare shop-ahead case; the on-hand check already catches it.
Not yours when it's a routine split of scope you've agreed.
5 Risk tolerance and trust
What the product defends against, who may bypass what, what's stored and for how long, and the privacy defaults.
  • #1419: account creation is refused from the EEA and the UK.
  • superheroes: findings about deliberately malicious input are outside the threat model; accepted, not fixed.
Not yours when your stated threat model already decides it.
6 Your own time, attention and habits
What reaches you, how and when, and what an agent may commit you to.
  • Nothing is filed without your word, Backlog included.
  • #1798: fixes from the advisor's vet come to you on a tap sheet before they're pushed.
Not yours when it's only how a message to you is worded; plain language is already the rule.
7 A case that tests or creates a principle
A case that clashes with a principle you've stated, or that no principle covers yet. Your answer becomes the principle: a standing ruling in Canon, which every later discovery reads.
  • #1419: one deletion dialog shared with household delete: "I really don't want to end up with multiple implementations of the same thing."
  • superheroes: a proposed approval gate clashed with your autonomy principle.
Not yours when a standing ruling in Canon plainly decides it.
8 The quality bar, and what counts as proof
Thresholds, what evidence comes before spending, and whether a known defect may ship.
  • This discovery: review rounds stop at 4, and anything still open comes to you.
  • superheroes: a merge was held for one more fix round instead of shipping known leftovers.
Not yours when the bar is set and the case clearly meets or misses it.
9 All user-facing copy
Every word the product shows its users. Agents draft it; you approve it. For superheroes, that's what the plugin says to its user.
  • #1798: "Need beyond Plans" replaced "not from a plan".
  • #1419: "Keep my account" instead of "Cancel", which could be misread as cancelling the subscription.
Not yours when it's instruction text agents read, under the prose standard.
10 Priority and timing
What ships when, when a decision gets made, and how much to invest now.
  • #1912 was held until the #1915 work was settled.
  • #1419: the terms documents wait until just before go-live.
Not yours when it's the order of build pieces inside an agreed stack.

How a project adjusts the line
The ten categories never change. What changes, project by project, is how many calls inside them are already answered.
  • Every ruling goes into the project's Canon as it's made, so the same question is never asked twice. Next time, it's "already answered."
  • When you cede a kind of call, "you decide these from now on," it goes into Canon as a standing ruling with an example, called a ceded call, and later calls of that kind are craft.
  • You can take any of them back with one word, as you did with filing.
