**Guarded element:** the `supersede` term in `validate_landing`'s store-exists gate —
`if exists and not supersede:` in `plugins/superheroes/lib/round_records.py`.

**Why this axis and not BP-B1's guard.** BP-B1 already proves the *refusal* bites. What this
end-to-end test adds beyond that refusal is the **second half** of the invariant: that the recovery
the refusal names actually works — a per-slot `--supersede --expect-sha256` replaces the stale

rather than the PLAUSIBLE that ingest silence would have produced. Neutralizing the supersede term
in the store-exists gate targets exactly that half: the refusal still fires and BP-B1's test stays
green, while this test goes red at the recovery step. Picking BP-B1's guard here instead would have
made the two proofs duplicates and left the recovery half unproven.

## Known limit of this record

The neutralization proves the test bites on the **recovery** half of its invariant. The test's
**fold** assertion (that `_fold_verifiers` applies CONFIRMED and the round's `verifyPasses` counts
`CONFIRMED: 1, PLAUSIBLE: 0`) is not separately neutralized here, because it is downstream of the
store contents this proof already moves — a stale store makes the test fail before the fold is
reached. That assertion is therefore covered as a *consequence* rather than by its own proof, and
is disclosed as such rather than claimed.
